# Native Windows title bar for the Hub, no custom chrome

**Superseded by [ADR-0003](0003-webview2-ui-supersedes-0001.md) (2026-09-29).** Kept for the history of the decision; v4 renders every surface in WebView2.

The Hub originally drew its own title bar: `WindowStyle="None"` plus a `WindowChrome` with the Aero caption buttons disabled, a 38px row styled in the Quiet Ledger palette, three hand-drawn minimize / maximize / close buttons with their own hover styles and click handlers, and a 1px outer border standing in for the frame Windows no longer painted. It kept the theme unbroken from the top edge of the window down, which is why it was built. We replaced it with WPF's default `SingleBorderWindow` style, so Windows draws the title bar, the icon and "VoiceCtrl" title, the caption buttons and the frame. We chose this because the custom bar looked and behaved like no other window on the desktop: the buttons weren't native, the maximize glyph never swapped to "restore", and Snap Layouts, the system menu, double-click-to-maximize and light/dark caption theming were all either missing or reimplemented by hand and drifting from the OS. The trade is real: the Quiet Ledger theme now stops at the body and the title bar takes whatever colour and height Windows gives it, and any future branding of that strip (accent colour, custom height, extra buttons) would mean rebuilding the chrome, its styles and hit-testing. That is consistent with ADR-0001's direction of keeping the Hub native rather than webview-like, and the native behaviours we get for free are worth more to a background utility than a themed caption strip.
